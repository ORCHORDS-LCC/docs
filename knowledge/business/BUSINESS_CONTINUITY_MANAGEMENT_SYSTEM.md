# Business Continuity Management System

## Overview

Business Continuity Management (BCM) ensures critical business functions continue during and after disruptive incidents. This document covers ISO 22301-aligned practices for organizational resilience.

## Key Components

### 1. Business Impact Analysis (BIA)
- Identify critical business functions and processes
- Determine Maximum Tolerable Downtime (MTD) for each function
- Establish Recovery Time Objectives (RTO) and Recovery Point Objectives (RPO)
- Map dependencies between functions, systems, and resources

### 2. Risk Assessment
- Identify threats: natural disasters, cyber attacks, supply chain failures, pandemics
- Assess likelihood and impact of each threat
- Prioritize risks based on BIA results
- Develop mitigation strategies for high-priority risks

### 3. Business Continuity Plan (BCP)
- Document response procedures for each scenario
- Define activation criteria and authority levels
- Establish communication protocols (internal/external)
- Identify alternate work locations and resources
- Define roles and responsibilities for crisis management team

### 4. Testing and Exercises
- Tabletop exercises: quarterly, scenario-based discussions
- Functional drills: semi-annual, testing specific procedures
- Full-scale simulations: annual, end-to-end recovery testing
- After-action reviews with corrective action tracking

### 5. Plan Maintenance
- Review BCP at least annually or after significant changes
- Update contact lists, resource inventories, and procedures
- Track corrective actions from tests and incidents
- Maintain version control and change logs

## Key Metrics

- **RTO**: Maximum acceptable recovery time per function
- **RPO**: Maximum acceptable data loss (time window)
- **MTPD**: Maximum Tolerable Period of Disruption
- **BCP coverage**: Percentage of critical functions with documented plans
- **Test completion rate**: Percentage of scheduled exercises completed
