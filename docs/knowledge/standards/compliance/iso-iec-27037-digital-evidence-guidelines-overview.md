## The "digital evidence" concept
Digital evidence is data of probative value that may be admitted in a legal proceeding.
ISO/IEC 27037 covers four phases:

- **Identification** — recognizing potential sources of digital evidence (VERIFIED from
  catalogue scope)
- **Collection** — gathering evidence while preserving its integrity (VERIFIED)
- **Acquisition** — obtaining digital data from devices and systems (VERIFIED)
- **Preservation** — maintaining evidence integrity from collection through legal
  proceedings (VERIFIED)

The standard applies to any organization handling digital evidence, including law
enforcement, corporate incident response teams, legal departments, and forensic
consultants.

## The "chain of custody" pattern
ISO/IEC 27037 establishes a chain-of-custody framework:

1. **Evidence identification** — locate and catalog potential evidence sources
2. **Evidence collection** — collect evidence using forensically sound methods
3. **Evidence acquisition** — create forensic copies of digital media
4. **Evidence preservation** — maintain integrity through storage, transfer, and legal
   proceedings

This pattern is VERIFIED from the catalogue scope. The standard provides guidelines,
not requirements; it does not mandate specific tools or techniques.

## Gotchas
- ISO/IEC 27037 is about *guidelines*, not legal admissibility. It does not determine
  whether evidence is admissible in a specific jurisdiction; it guides how to handle it.
- The standard covers digital evidence broadly — not just network forensics but also
  storage media, mobile devices, cloud environments, and IoT devices.
- The standard was published in 2012 and confirmed in 2018. No revision has been issued
  as of the catalogue verification date.
