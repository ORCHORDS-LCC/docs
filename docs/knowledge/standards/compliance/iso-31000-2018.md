# ISO 31000:2018 — Risk management — Guidelines

**Issue:** The corpus has no document on ISO 31000, the risk management standard that most governance and audit programmes are expected to align with.
**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Author:** ORCHORDS
**Status:** documented

## Symptom
An organisation runs a risk programme that no external framework recognises. There is no document in the corpus that states what ISO 31000 requires, what it deliberately leaves out, or the most common way it is misapplied — which is to treat it as a compliance checklist rather than a framework.

## Root cause
ISO 31000 is the ISO's guidance standard for risk management. It provides principles, a framework and a process rather than requirements, and it is explicitly **not** a certifiable standard. That distinction is the source of most of its misuse.

**Source:** ISO catalogue entry:
https://www.iso.org/standard/65694.html

## The "principles, framework and process" concept
ISO 31000 is built on three elements and it uses the same PDCA cycle as the other management-system standards:

- **Principles** — the underlying beliefs and behaviours that make risk management effective, such as that risk is managed where it arises and that risk management is value-adding.
- **Framework** — the structure that lets an organisation customise the process to its own context and appetite, with no prescribed internal structure.
- **Process** — the application of the principles and framework to a specific risk, giving objectives, actions and outcomes. The process is deliberately non-prescriptive; ISO 31000 does not mandate a particular technique.

Because the framework is customisable, two organisations can both be "aligned to ISO 31000" and have completely different risk processes. A statement that an organisation "follows ISO 31000" therefore carries very little information on its own.

The full text is paywalled by ISO. This document is grounded in the official catalogue entry and the abstract published on it.

## Gotchas
- **ISO 31000 is not certifiable.** ISO states plainly that it does not lead to risk management certification. Any claim that an organisation is "ISO 31000 certified" is wrong. Certification against ISO 31000 is sometimes offered by private bodies and has no basis in the standard.
- **Do not cite "ISO 31000:2009" as current.** The 2018 edition is Edition 2 and is the current one; the 2009 edition was Edition 1.
- The standard does not require a specific risk methodology, register format, or scoring scheme. If a document claims ISO 31000 mandates a particular technique, that claim is wrong.
- It covers risk in general — not only information security risk. For information security risk specifically, the relevant instruments are ISO/IEC 27005 and the risk-management guidance in ISO/IEC 23894.
- It is not an information-security standard despite being widely used in that context; it will not tell you which controls to select.
