# ISO/IEC 27019:2024 — Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection — Information security controls for the energy utility industry

**Issue:** The corpus has no document on ISO/IEC 27019, the security controls for the energy utility industry.
**Date:** 2026-10-29
**Author:** ORCHORDS
**Status:** documented

## Symptom
An organisation in power, gas, oil, heat or electricity transmission has no reference in the corpus for the security controls the energy sector is expected to apply, what scope those controls cover, or which control catalogue they are derived from.

## Root cause
ISO/IEC 27019 gives information security controls for the energy utility industry, based on ISO/IEC 27002:2022, for controlling and monitoring the production or generation, transmission, storage and distribution of electric power, gas, oil and heat, and for the control of associated supporting processes.

**Source:** ISO catalogue entry:
https://www.iso.org/standard/85056.html

## The "energy utility controls" concept
The scope extends well beyond the operational technology itself. ISO/IEC 27019 explicitly covers:

- central and distributed process control, monitoring and automation technology, and the information systems used to operate it, including programming and parameterisation devices;
- digital controllers and automation components — control and field devices, programmable logic controllers, digital sensor and actuator elements;
- the supporting information systems in the process control domain, including supplementary data visualisation, control and monitoring, data archiving, historian logging, reporting and documentation;
- communication technology in the process control domain — networks, telemetry, telecontrol applications and remote-control technology;
- advanced metering infrastructure components, including smart meters;
- measurement devices, including emission values;
- digital protection and safety systems — protection relays, safety PLCs, emergency governor mechanisms;
- energy management systems, including distributed energy resources, electric charging infrastructure, and domestic premises with private installations;
- distributed components of smart grid environments.

This is what makes the standard a sector profile rather than a technology standard: it inherits ISO/IEC 27002:2022 and reinterprets it for a sector whose failure modes are physical rather than purely informational.

## Verification
- ISO/IEC 27019:2024 — Published, Edition 2, 2024-10, ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 27. Catalogue page: `https://www.iso.org/standard/85056.html`
- Published 2024-10-18, following approval as a final draft (stage 50.20) on 2024-09-06.
- Edition 1 was ISO/IEC 27019:2017, which was based on ISO/IEC 27002:2013.

The full text is paywalled by ISO. This document is grounded in the official catalogue entry and the abstract published on it.

## Gotchas
- **The official title carries the "Information security, cybersecurity and privacy protection —" prefix**, introduced when the ISO/IEC 27000 family was renamed. The 2017 edition was titled "Information technology — Security techniques — ...". Quoting the old prefix against the 2024 edition is a title mismatch.
- **The 2024 edition is based on ISO/IEC 27002:2022.** The 2017 edition was based on ISO/IEC 27002:2013. A control identifier from one edition does not automatically mean the same thing in the other.
- This is a sector profile, not a general security standard. It applies to the energy utility industry; it does not apply to data centres, software vendors or general enterprises.
- It is not an ICS security standard. It does not address the protocol security of a specific industrial control system, and it does not cover safety instrumented systems beyond the digital protection and safety systems listed above.
- ISO/IEC 27019 does not replace the operational and safety regulations of any jurisdiction. It is a controls document, not a compliance instrument.
